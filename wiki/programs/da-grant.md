EGD's non-repayable grant voucher for buying a certified digital solution from a DSP, including end-user training. Up to $400 for formal enterprises and $250 for informal ones. The enterprise pays the DSP first, and TSC reimburses it after progress reports.

**Khmer:** Digital Adoption (DA) Grant (ជំនួយថវិកាគាំទ្រការចាប់យកឌីជីថល (DA Grant)), Glossary 5.1. Grant Voucher (បណ្ណជំនួយថវិកា (Grant Voucher)), Glossary 5.2.

## What it is

- **KB §5:** "provides financial support to help enterprises adopt digital solutions. Business owners receive a voucher that can be used to purchase certified digital tools and receive end-user training."
- **Guidebook ¶1:** the grant speeds up digital adoption by giving financial support "in the form of a Grant Voucher". The business owner can use it to adopt digital solutions and receive training in using them.
- **Guidebook ¶5:** a Grant Voucher is "a small grant given under set conditions that **does not have to be repaid**".
- **No cash handout:** FAQ 6.3 says "No direct cash is provided! … the EGD Program does not provide direct cash handouts". The support comes through the voucher, and TSC reimburses spending already made on an approved solution (KB §5; GB ¶17). Both statements hold together: you get no upfront cash, only reimbursement against real spending.

Goals (GB ¶2):

- promote digital adoption;
- ease the early financial burden that holds owners back;
- build owners' digital skills through the training included in the DSP's **Digital Solution Package**.

## Grant amounts

| Enterprise type | Maximum grant amount | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Formal Economy (Registered) | $400 (or 1,600,000 Riels) | KB §5; Brief §2.5; Glossary 5.3 |
| Informal Economy (Unregistered / Micro) | $250 (or 1,000,000 Riels) | KB §5; Brief §2.5; Glossary 5.4 |

Quote these exactly; see [[official-figures]].

- **Top-ups:** "If the cost of the selected digital solution exceeds the grant limit, the enterprise is responsible for paying the remaining amount" (KB §5; GB ¶12).
- **More than one voucher:** allowed, "provided that the total value of all vouchers does not exceed the maximum allowable limit" (KB §5). GB ¶11 adds that each voucher must be for a **different** digital-solution need.

> **Differs from the Guidebook** ([[source-conflicts]] #7). The 2024 Guidebook (¶11–12) states one maximum for every business owner: 400 US dollars or ១ ៦០០ ០០០ riels per voucher, with no lower informal tier. The $250 informal cap appears only in the 2026 chatbot documents. Answer with the 2026 two-tier figures.

> **Registered micro enterprises.** The KB's $250 row says "Unregistered / Micro". The Glossary (5.4) says only "unregistered/informal". The Guidebook (¶9) defines the formal track as **legally registered** businesses. So the tier follows **registration status, not size**: a registered micro enterprise is formal ($400). This is an inference; it's recorded on [[source-gaps]] for EGD to confirm.

**Amounts can change:** vouchers depend on the funding TSC actually receives and the donor approves. TSC may raise the maximum or the validity based on donor funding, an assessment of the owner, government guidance and the relevant regulations (GB ¶11–12).

## Validity and processing

- **Validity:** "valid for up to 12 months" (KB §5; Brief §2.5), "from the date of issuance" (Glossary 5.5; GB ¶12). An unused voucher **expires automatically** at the end of the period (GB ¶12).
- **Processing time:** "maximum 2 working days" (KB §5; Glossary 5.6). GB ¶14: TSC reviews the application within at most 2 working days of receiving it. The website says the same: "យ៉ាងយូរ ២ ថ្ងៃធ្វើការ".

## Who can apply

- **KB §5:** "Business owners who want to adopt digital solutions."
- **Priority** (KB §5; GB ¶7) goes to enterprises that:
  - have never used digital solutions before;
  - need digital tools to expand their market;
  - are in government priority sectors.
- **Others** are assessed "based on the actual situation of the business owner or business activity" (GB ¶7).

These conditions come from the 2024 Guidebook only; the 2026 KB doesn't state them. Cite them as Guidebook conditions.

### Is DA Training required?

GB ¶6 says both beneficiary types, business owners and DSPs, "**must participate in**" an EGD digital adoption course. It also says those who haven't "will be supported following general or special principles based on necessity". Self-employed people are supported based on an actual assessment.

The KB (§4) lists grant eligibility as a *benefit* of [[da-training]].

**Answer:** taking a DA Training course is the expected route and makes applying simpler (GB ¶13; see [[da-grant-application]]). It isn't an absolute bar: applicants who haven't trained are considered case by case. Recommend the [[digital-adoption-assessment]] and a DA course first.

## What the grant can't pay for

- **KB §5:** "The DA Grant cannot be used for hardware, equipment, network infrastructure, or human resources."
- **GB ¶19:** beneficiaries bear their own costs of connecting to the EGD Platform and of their own IT: network infrastructure, electronic equipment and human resources. "The voucher is not used for this purpose."

## Payment and reimbursement (KB §5; GB ¶16–17)

1. **The enterprise pays the DSP first**, within the voucher's amount and validity as approved by TSC or agreed with the DSP.
2. It **starts using** the solution after officially receiving the voucher.
3. It **submits progress reports** to TSC showing the solution's impact or outputs.
4. **TSC reimburses** the enterprise after receiving the progress reports, by stage of actual implementation.

**Stopping early:** to stop using the solution before the end of the voucher period, notify TSC with clear reasons, then wait for TSC's assessment to close the payment (GB ¶17).

Full steps: [[da-grant-application]].

## Reporting and follow-up (GB ¶16, ¶20)

- Progress reports are due **in stages, from 3 months onward** after starting to use the solution. They focus on measurable results such as sales, productivity and costs. ("ចាប់ពី ៣ ខែឡើងទៅ", checked against the page image.)
- Beneficiaries cooperate in surveys on challenges and improvements, and can receive further support after evaluation.
- TSC compiles these reports for the donor, the [[ministry-of-economy-and-finance]] and other parties.

## Terms everyone accepts

Submitting an application means accepting the Guidebook's **General Conditions** (GB ¶13). Key points: TSC isn't responsible for the DSP's quality; the business owns its data; disputes are between the business and the DSP. See [[da-grant-general-conditions]].
