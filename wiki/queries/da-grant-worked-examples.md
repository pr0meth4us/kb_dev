Seven worked scenarios showing how the DA Grant rules apply: amounts, top-ups, multiple vouchers, ineligible items, expiry, stopping early and reimbursement. The scenarios are illustrative. The rules and figures are from [[da-grant]] and [[da-grant-application]].

**Rules used** (exact figures from [[official-figures]]):
- **Maximum:** $400 (or 1,600,000 Riels) formal; $250 (or 1,000,000 Riels) informal.
- **Top-ups:** above the limit, the enterprise pays the rest (KB §5; GB ¶12).
- **Multiple vouchers:** allowed if the total stays within the maximum (KB §5), each for a different solution need (GB ¶11).
- **Not covered:** hardware, equipment, network infrastructure or human resources (KB §5).
- **Validity and processing:** valid for up to 12 months from issuance, then expires automatically (Glossary 5.5; GB ¶12); processing maximum 2 working days.
- **Payment:** pay the DSP first; TSC reimburses after progress reports (KB §5; GB ¶17).

## 1. A registered café buys a POS system costing $600

- Registered and operating for over 6 months → formal tier → maximum **$400 (or 1,600,000 Riels)**.
- POS is an eligible category.
- The café pays the DSP. TSC's reimbursement is capped at the voucher amount, at most $400. **The café bears the remaining $200 itself** (KB §5: "the enterprise is responsible for paying the remaining amount").

## 2. A market-stall vendor (unregistered) wants an e-payment tool costing $180

- Informal tier → maximum **$250 (or 1,000,000 Riels)**. $180 is within the limit.
- Needs: a clear purpose for the voucher, and proof of operation such as an informal-economy recognition certificate or a sub-national one-window certificate (GB ¶9).
- **No top-up needed.** The voucher can cover the full $180 if approved.

## 3. A registered shop wants two solutions: digital marketing ($250) and inventory management ($200)

- Total $450 is more than the $400 maximum.
- Two vouchers are allowed only if their **total stays within $400** and each meets a different need (KB §5; GB ¶11). For example, $250 + $150, with the shop paying the remaining $50 on inventory.
- The two needs differ, so the "different need" condition is met.

## 4. A business wants the grant to buy a tablet and a POS app

- The **POS app** is eligible.
- The **tablet** is hardware, so it isn't covered: "The DA Grant cannot be used for hardware, equipment, network infrastructure, or human resources" (KB §5; GB ¶19).
- Only the software solution counts towards the voucher.

## 5. A voucher issued in January is still unused in the following January

- It is valid for up to 12 months from issuance (Glossary 5.5), then **expires automatically** if unused (GB ¶12).
- The sources don't say whether a new application can be made after expiry. Refer that question to [[contacts]].

## 6. An enterprise stops using the solution after 4 months

- It must **notify TSC with clear reasons** and wait for TSC's assessment to close the payment (GB ¶17).
- Reimbursement is "by stage of actual implementation" (GB ¶17). Expect it to reflect only the stages reported. The exact amount isn't set out in the sources.

## 7. When does the enterprise get its money back?

- Sequence: pay the DSP → start using the solution → submit progress reports (in stages, from 3 months onward, on measurable results such as sales, productivity and costs) → TSC reimburses (GB ¶16–17, ¶20).
- **How long TSC takes to reimburse after a report isn't stated** ([[source-gaps]]). Don't promise a date.

## Common misunderstandings

| Statement | Correct? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "EGD gives me $400 cash." | No | No direct cash; reimbursement against real spending (FAQ 6.3; GB ¶17) |
| "I must finish DA Training to apply." | Not strictly | Expected (GB ¶6) and simplifies the application (GB ¶13); non-participants are assessed case by case |
| "The grant is a loan." | No | A voucher is a small grant that "does not have to be repaid" (GB ¶5) |
| "TSC guarantees the DSP's work." | No | TSC isn't responsible for quality or delivery; disputes are between the business and the DSP ([[da-grant-general-conditions]]) |
| "Applying costs a fee." | No | No enrolment fees or commissions (KB §1) |
